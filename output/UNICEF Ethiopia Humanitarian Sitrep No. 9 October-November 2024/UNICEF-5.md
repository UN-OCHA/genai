## Highlights
- Over 9 million children are out of school due to conflict, violence, natural disasters, and displacement.
- More than 6,000 schools have closed, and approximately 10,000 schools were damaged due to climate shocks and conflict.
- Over 9 million malaria cases, including 1,267 deaths, were reported from Oromia, Amhara, South Ethiopia, and Southwest regions.
- UNICEF provided lifesaving treatment to more than 107,000 acutely malnourished children under five years of age.
- Access to safe drinking water was provided to over 571,000 people.
- UNICEF requires US$ 535 million for humanitarian assistance, with a 76% funding gap.

## Funding Overview and Partnerships
- UNICEF’s Humanitarian Action for Children (HAC) 2024 Appeal requires US$535.3 million.
- To date, UNICEF Ethiopia has received US$95 million, with a 76% funding gap.
- Donors include CERF, Denmark, UK Aid, US BHA, US PRM, Japan, Canada, Ireland, Norway, Germany, Sweden, Denmark, and private sector donors.

## Situation Overview and Humanitarian Needs
- Ongoing conflict in Ethiopia, particularly in Amhara and Oromia regions, impacts public health.
- Cholera outbreak active in 18 woredas from six regions.
- Malaria has reached epidemic proportions with over 9 million cases.
- Flooding in Shashego and Gelana woredas displaced thousands and damaged farmland.
- Education sector devastated with 9 million children out of school.
- Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) prevalence of 17.1% in Gambella region.

## Summary Analysis of Programme Response
### Health
- 713,798 women and children accessed primary health care services.
- Over 30,000 children received measles vaccinations.
- 310,000 individuals received cholera vaccines.
- Essential health services provided in various regions.

### Nutrition
- Lifesaving therapeutic and preventative nutrition services provided to IDPs, refugees, and vulnerable populations.
- Over 107,000 children treated for Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM).
- Vitamin A supplementation and iron folic acid supplementation provided.

### WASH
- Over 571,000 people accessed safe drinking water.
- 44,800 people gained access to sanitation facilities.
- Hygiene and sanitation campaigns reached thousands.

### Child Protection
- 72,640 children and 34,947 adults reached through child protection and GBV interventions.
- MHPSS services provided to 19,259 individuals.
- Family tracing and reunification services offered.

### Education
- Nearly 48,000 children accessed formal and non-formal education services.
- Over 103,000 children received learning materials.
- Training provided to teachers and facilitators.

### Social Protection
- 2,923 conflict-affected households received cash transfers.
- Financial literacy and business skills training provided.

### Social and Behavioural Change
- Over 17 million people reached through outreach and mobilization efforts.
- Feedback collected from over 13,000 people.

### Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse
- Over 39,000 individuals reached with information on SEA reporting channels.
- PSEA capacity assessments conducted.

## Human Interest Stories and External Media
- UNICEF hosted UK Minister for Africa and highlighted lifesaving services.
- Videos and stories published on nutrition, education, and health interventions.

## Humanitarian Leadership, Coordination and Strategy
- UNICEF’s strategy aligns with the 2024 HRP.
- UNICEF leads Nutrition and WASH Clusters and Child Protection Area of Responsibility.
- Active participation in OCHA-led coordination mechanisms.

# Summary
The UNICEF Ethiopia Humanitarian Situation Report for October-November 2024 highlights the severe impact of conflict, natural disasters, and displacement on children and families in Ethiopia. Over 9 million children are out of school, and more than 9 million malaria cases have been reported. UNICEF has provided critical health, nutrition, WASH, and protection services, reaching millions of people. Despite these efforts, a significant funding gap of 76% remains, with US$ 535 million required for continued humanitarian assistance. The report emphasizes the need for targeted interventions to address acute malnutrition, improve access to education, and enhance child protection and social services. UNICEF's strategy aligns with the 2024 HRP, focusing on coordination and collaboration with partners to maximize impact.
