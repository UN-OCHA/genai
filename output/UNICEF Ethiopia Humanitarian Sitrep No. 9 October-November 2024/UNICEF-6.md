## Highlights
- Over 9 million children are out of school due to conflict, violence, natural disasters, and displacement.
- More than 6,000 schools have closed, and approximately 10,000 schools were damaged due to climate shocks and conflict.
- Over 9 million malaria cases, including 1,267 deaths, were reported from Oromia, Amhara, South Ethiopia, and Southwest regions.
- UNICEF provided lifesaving treatment to more than 107,000 acutely malnourished children under five.
- Access to safe drinking water was provided to over 571,000 people.
- UNICEF requires US$ 535 million for humanitarian assistance, with a 76% funding gap.

## Funding Overview and Partnerships
- UNICEF's Humanitarian Action for Children (HAC) 2024 Appeal requires US$535.3 million.
- UNICEF Ethiopia has received US$95 million towards the appeal, with a 76% funding gap.
- Donors include CERF, Denmark, UK Aid, US BHA, US PRM, Japan, Canada, Ireland, Norway, Germany, Sweden, Denmark, and private sector donors.

## Situation Overview and Humanitarian Needs
- The ongoing conflict in Ethiopia, particularly in Amhara and Oromia regions, is impacting public health.
- Cholera outbreak is active in 18 woredas from six regions.
- Malaria has reached epidemic proportions with over 9 million cases.
- Heavy unseasonal rainfall caused flooding in Shashego and Gelana woredas.
- Approximately 9 million children are out of school due to conflict and natural disasters.

## Summary Analysis of Programme Response
### Health
- 713,798 women and children accessed primary health care services.
- Over 30,000 children received measles vaccinations.
- Over 310,000 individuals received cholera vaccines.
- Essential health services provided in various regions.

### Nutrition
- Over 107,000 children received treatment for SAM.
- Over 729,000 children received vitamin A supplementation.
- SAM admissions increased in Tigray, Amhara, and other regions.

### WASH
- Over 571,000 people accessed safe drinking water.
- 44,800 people gained access to sanitation facilities.
- Over 91,000 people received basic WASH non-food items.

### Child Protection
- 72,640 children and 34,947 adults reached through child protection and GBV interventions.
- 19,259 individuals received MHPSS services.
- 1,285 unaccompanied and separated children received care services.

### Education
- Nearly 48,000 children accessed formal and non-formal education services.
- Over 103,000 children received essential learning materials.

### Social Protection
- 2,923 conflict-affected households received Shock-Responsive Cash Transfers.
- Community service workers linked individuals to various services.

### Social and Behavioural Change (SBC) and Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)
- Over 17 million people reached through outreach and mobilization efforts.
- Feedback collected from over 13,000 people regarding services provided.

### Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)
- Over 39,000 individuals reached with information on SEA reporting channels.
- Partner PSEA capacity assessments conducted.

## Human Interest Stories and External Media
- UNICEF hosted UK Minister for Africa and highlighted various humanitarian efforts.
- Published videos and stories on nutrition, education, and protection efforts.

## Humanitarian Leadership, Coordination, and Strategy
- UNICEF's strategy aligns with the 2024 HRP and cluster priorities.
- Active participation in coordination mechanisms with UN, NGOs, and the Government of Ethiopia.

# Summary
The UNICEF Ethiopia report for October-November 2024 highlights the severe humanitarian challenges faced by the country due to conflict, natural disasters, and health crises. Over 9 million children are out of school, and the country is grappling with a significant malaria outbreak. UNICEF has been actively providing health, nutrition, WASH, education, and protection services, reaching millions of people. Despite these efforts, there is a substantial funding gap of 76% in UNICEF's humanitarian appeal. The report underscores the need for continued support and coordination among various stakeholders to address the urgent needs of affected populations.
